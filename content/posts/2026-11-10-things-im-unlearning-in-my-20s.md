Your twenties are strange.

One moment, you feel like you have everything figured out, and the next, you're questioning every decision you've ever made. You're trying to build a career, maintain relationships, take care of yourself, make money, explore the world and somehow enjoy the process too.

And somewhere in between all of this, you start realising that growing up isn't just about learning new things. It's also about unlearning a few.

Here are some things I'm slowly trying to unlearn.

**1. Thinking I need to have everything figured out**

There's this unspoken pressure in your twenties to know exactly what you're doing with your life.

What career are you pursuing? Where do you see yourself in five years? Are you earning enough? Are you successful yet?

Sometimes, it feels like everyone around you has a plan, and you're the only one still figuring things out.

But I've started realising that life isn't a competition with a fixed deadline. It's okay to change your mind, explore different interests, make mistakes and take a different route.

You don't need to have your entire life planned at 23, 25 or even 29.

You're allowed to figure it out as you go.

**2. Comparing my journey with someone else's**

Social media makes this one particularly difficult.

You open Instagram and see someone travelling abroad, someone buying a house, someone getting promoted, someone getting married, and suddenly your own life starts feeling a little less exciting.

But we're comparing our everyday lives with carefully selected moments from someone else's.

We rarely see the struggles, the uncertainty, the sacrifices or the time it took to get there.

I've realised that someone else's success doesn't take anything away from mine. Their timeline doesn't have to become my timeline.

I'd rather focus on building a life that I genuinely enjoy than spend my time trying to catch up with someone else's.

**3. Believing that saying no makes me a bad person**

For a long time, I thought being a good person meant being available, accommodating and understanding all the time.

Saying yes even when I didn't want to. Adjusting even when I was uncomfortable. Avoiding difficult conversations because I didn't want someone to be upset with me.

But constantly keeping everyone happy can come at the cost of your own peace.

I'm learning that boundaries aren't disrespectful. You can care about someone and still disagree with them. You can love people and still need space. You can say no without writing an entire explanation to justify yourself.

Not everyone has to agree with your decisions for them to be valid.

**4. Thinking productivity determines my worth**

There are days when I feel incredibly productive, get things done, tick off my to-do list and feel like I'm making progress.

And then there are days when I just want to do nothing.

I'm slowly learning that both kinds of days are allowed.

Resting doesn't mean you're lazy. Taking a break doesn't mean you've lost your ambition. And having an unproductive day doesn't mean you're falling behind in life.

We spend so much time trying to become better versions of ourselves that sometimes we forget to appreciate the person we already are.

I want to be ambitious without making my entire identity revolve around how much I achieve.

**5. Expecting everyone to understand me**

One thing adulthood has taught me is that not everyone will understand your intentions, your decisions or even your silence.

And honestly, that's okay.

Sometimes, you can explain yourself perfectly and someone may still interpret things differently. You can have the best intentions and still disappoint someone.

I'm learning that I don't have to spend all my energy convincing people of who I am.

Of course, communication matters. Taking accountability matters. But there's a difference between explaining yourself and constantly seeking approval.

I'd rather be at peace with the person I know myself to be than exhaust myself trying to control everyone's perception of me.

**6. Taking every little thing personally**

A delayed reply, a cancelled plan, someone's changed mood or a conversation that didn't go as expected.

It's easy to overthink these things and immediately assume that something is wrong.

But not everything is about us.

People have their own responsibilities, bad days, worries and things happening behind the scenes. Sometimes, a situation is simply what it is, without a hidden meaning.

I'm learning to pause before jumping to conclusions, communicate when something genuinely bothers me and not let every small inconvenience disturb my entire day.

A little less overthinking can make life so much lighter.

**7. Believing that starting over means failing**

This might be one of the most important things I'm learning.

Sometimes, you outgrow certain habits, friendships, environments, goals or even versions of yourself.

And that's not necessarily a bad thing.

You can change your career direction. You can pick up a hobby you abandoned. You can rebuild your routine, start taking care of yourself again or decide that something you once wanted isn't what you want anymore.

Starting again doesn't erase the progress you've made.

In fact, sometimes it takes more courage to admit that something isn't working and choose a different path.

I want to get comfortable with beginning again, without treating every new beginning like evidence that I did something wrong.

**A little something before you go :)**

I don't think growing up means becoming someone who has all the answers.

Maybe it means becoming someone who is more comfortable with questions.

Someone who knows when to hold on and when to let go. Someone who can celebrate others without questioning their own worth. Someone who understands that life is allowed to change direction.

I'm still learning, still making mistakes and still figuring out what works for me.

But I think that's the beauty of being in your twenties. You get to discover who you are, not just through the things you learn, but also through the things you finally decide to leave behind.

> **And perhaps the goal isn't to have everything figured out.**
>
> **It's to become a little more comfortable being yourself along the way.**
